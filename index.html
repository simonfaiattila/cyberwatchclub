What this page is about

This page tracks two things equally, not one as the headline and one as an afterthought: how far the robotaxi race has actually gotten (Tesla, Waymo, and Zoox, side by side — not just Tesla's version of the story), and whether the "in-car monetization" idea (ads or sponsorships paying for rides) turns into a real product or stays speculation. Every item below is labeled verified or speculative, so the two don't get blurred together.

Tesla unveiled the Cybercab in October 2024 and launched a Model Y/Model 3-based robotaxi service in Austin in June 2025, since expanded to Dallas and Houston. Waymo and Zoox are further along by most public metrics (rides, fleet size, city count) — the comparison table below spells out the gap rather than assuming Tesla is ahead by default.

This is not a spotting app — it won't tell you where to catch a ride or log a sighting. For that, robotaxitracker.com (community license-plate and ride-receipt tracking) and basenor.com (Tesla accessories with a robotaxi news blog) both do that well. This page exists for the angle they don't cover as a primary focus: the stock, the multi-operator scale gap, and the monetization story, in one place.

Everything on this page comes from one of four places: (1) a live TSLA price feed, (2) a GitHub Action that checks the news every 6 hours and drops fresh headlines into the "Live feed," (3) a small number of hand-verified figures (fleet size, city status) that get updated when a new number is confirmed, and (4) federal safety filings — every US operator of a self-driving system is legally required to report crashes to NHTSA, which is the single most credible data source used here. Nothing is scraped from social media or presented as fact without a link back to where it came from.

Global context (Apollo Go's footprint in China and the Gulf) and a broader AI/robotics stock watchlist are included too, on the idea that the robotaxi race doesn't move in isolation from the chips, compute, and automation companies underneath it.

    Safety incident tracker — NHTSA-mandated crash reports

    Every company operating an Automated Driving System (ADS) on US public roads is legally required to report crashes to NHTSA under Standing General Order 2021-01, within 1–10 days of the event. This is the highest-credibility data source available — a mandatory federal filing, not a press release.

      Common questions

      Is Tesla's robotaxi bigger than Waymo's?
      No — not yet, by any public metric. Waymo runs in 11 cities with roughly 3,500 vehicles and about 500,000 paid rides a week; Tesla's reported fleet was 59 vehicles across 3 cities as of June 2026.

      Does TSLA stock move on robotaxi news?
      It can — expansion announcements, safety-monitor removal, and analyst notes on monetization have all coincided with price moves. This page pairs the live TSLA chart with a dated list of robotaxi events so you can check that for yourself, rather than take a headline's word for it.

      What is "in-car advertising" for robotaxis?
      The idea that ads or sponsorships shown on a robotaxi's screen could subsidize or replace the fare. No operator has an official program as of this writing — the $472B-by-2030 figure above is a UBS market estimate, not a product announcement.
